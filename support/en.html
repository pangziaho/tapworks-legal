Questions or problems? Email softnjer@yeah.net. Please include your iPhone model, iOS version and the card or tag you were reading.

Common questions

Which cards can TapWorks read?

China T-Union cards and many city cards (Shanghai, Shenzhen Tong, Wuhan Tong, Lingnan Pass/Yang Cheng Tong and others), Hong Kong Octopus (balance only), Japanese IC cards such as Suica, PASMO and ICOCA, Rakuten Edy, nanaco, WAON, and Korean T-money and Cashbee. It also reads and writes NFC tags such as NTAG213/215/216. The full list is in Settings › Supported Cards.

Where do I hold the card?

Tap Read Card, then hold the card flat against the top back of the iPhone, near the camera, for a second or two. Remove metal or very thick cases, and use one card at a time.

My access card or hotel key does nothing.

Most access cards and hotel keys use MIFARE Classic chips, which iPhone does not support, so there is no response. Apple does not let apps emulate cards, so no app can copy an access card to an iPhone. You can still add the card manually to keep its number and photos.

Can it read bank cards or passports?

No. iOS does not let apps read bank cards, and TapWorks does not read passports to protect your personal data.

Why are Japanese stations shown as codes?

The databases that map station codes to names do not allow commercial use. Tap a ride and give the station a name; it is shown for that code on all your cards from then on.

My Octopus balance is HK$15 lower than it should be.

Octopus cards store the balance plus the allowed negative value, which changed for cards issued from October 2017. The issue date can’t be read, so new cards are assumed. Open the card and turn on “Issued before October 2017”.

My T-Union card shows a different balance than the machine.

TapWorks subtracts the overdraft allowance that some cities add to the available amount. Cards that combine a T-Union wallet with a local wallet have two separate balances; both are shown.

Can it read cards in Apple Wallet?

No. Cards in Apple Wallet are emulated by the phone, and an iPhone can’t read its own Wallet cards. Check their balance in the Wallet app.

Which tags should I buy for writing?

NTAG213 (144 bytes) is enough for a web link or Wi-Fi; NTAG215 (504 bytes) or NTAG216 (888 bytes) for business cards and several records. Write lets you lock a tag, set a write password, write many tags in a row and save templates.

How do I run a shortcut with a tag?

Open Shortcuts › Automation › + › NFC, scan any tag and choose what to run. The automation only uses the tag’s ID, so nothing needs to be written to it.

How do I move my cards to a new iPhone?

Turn on Settings › Back Up to iCloud Drive Automatically, or choose Export Backup File. On the new iPhone, open the backup file or choose Restore from Backup.
